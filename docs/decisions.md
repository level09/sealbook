# Engineering decisions

| Decision | Reason and boundary |
| --- | --- |
| PostgreSQL remains the evidence substrate | Custody, permissions, policy, search and jobs share transactions; no second truth store |
| Signed statements remain the factual write path | New search features produce leads or proposals, never direct person-row edits |
| Two fingerprint layers | Picture catches visual reuse; sound catches reuse after crop or compression. Missing sound is explicit |
| Embeddings serve discovery | Actor profiles and text can be similar without depicting the same identity or independent origin |
| Hosted zero-retention models by default, no fallback (2026-10-25) | OCR, extraction, agents and embeddings run on OpenRouter with `provider.zdr`, strong models with a strict zero-retention endpoint (since 2026-11-22: Haiku 5.5, GPT-6.2 Sol, GPT-6 Luna, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, text-embedding-3-large; rows below). Small local models read Arabic registers badly and few deployments run their own GPU server; one that does hosts a private OpenAI-compatible endpoint (RunPod, Modal, Ollama) and sets it as the local route, which then wins for every source. A failed route is still shown, never silently swapped for another |
| Pinned zero-retention providers, checked after the call (2026-10-28) | `ROUTE_CLOUD_ZDR_PROVIDERS` sends `provider.only` with `provider.zdr`, and the served provider each response names is checked against it, because a routing preference alone is not evidence of who served. Display names are compared to slugs by folding both to lowercase letters and digits; a name that does not fold to its slug fails rather than passes. Empty pins nothing |
| Hosted speech to text for ordinary recordings, ElevenLabs Scribe v2 (2026-11-22) | Ordinary recordings go to `TRANSCRIBE_MODEL` (`elevenlabs/scribe-v2`) on OpenRouter's speech to text endpoint with `provider.zdr`; sensitive ones stay on the worker's Whisper, which the general sensitive row lists first, as sensitive scans do for OCR (`sealbook_0006`). The local model is the route when no hosted model is set; a failed hosted call fails visibly and is not rerouted. Measured on 18 clips from the repo (six synthetic voice notes with known scripts and sentence times, one English and five Damascene Arabic, and eleven clips with no speech: the demo's silent clip and ten old-footage clips of water and rain), numbers folded to the scripts' spelling: Scribe v2 word error 0.115 (English 0.037, Arabic 0.123), no word on any no-speech clip (it tags "[background noise]" instead), word times within 160 ms median of the script's sentence edges, under a second a call, USD 0.0037 a minute. AssemblyAI Universal 3.6 Pro: 0.139, no invented word, 65 ms, USD 0.005. Local Whisper large-v3-turbo: 0.146, 17 invented words on two water clips, 445 ms, about 30 s of CPU a 30 s clip. Mistral Voxtral Small Transcribe 3.0: 0.153 and invented a sentence, segment times only. Nvidia Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe: 0.229, invented words. Whisper large-v3 hosted: 74 invented words in Japanese, Portuguese and English. Deepgram Nova-3 read no Arabic. Google Chirp 3 (no times, invented) and the two without a zero-retention endpoint, OpenAI GPT Transcribe (0.111, no times) and Gemini 3.5 Transcribe (0.191, invented), were measured but cannot be the default. Facts relied on, checked 2026-11-22: OpenRouter lists an ElevenLabs zero-retention endpoint for `elevenlabs/scribe-v2` at USD 0.0000611 an audio second (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/endpoints/zdr); `zdr: true` restricts routing to endpoints that keep no prompts, and self-serve accounts also allow "Safety Retention" endpoints unless the account is set to ZDR only (https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/zdr.md); the speech to text response names neither the served model nor the provider, so the run records the requested model and the call's generation id, which OpenRouter's generation record resolves to the model version and provider some seconds later. Reverse it if the ElevenLabs endpoint leaves the zero-retention list, if a zero-retention model with word times beats it on this set, or if the response stops returning word times |
| Only strict zero-retention models, no Google model for now (2026-11-22) | Every hosted model must have a zero-retention endpoint without "Safety Retention", because the owner's OpenRouter account is set to ZDR only and refuses the others, and the site promises a sensitive source goes only to a private model or a zero-retention route. On 2026-11-22 every zero-retention endpoint of `google/gemini-4-argon`, `google/gemini-3.8-flash` and `google/gemini-embedding-2` carried `safety_retention: true` and each call answered 404 "No endpoints found matching your data policy". `tests/test_zdr_models.py` (runs where a key is set) reads OpenRouter's public endpoint list and fails when a configured hosted model has no endpoint with `safety_retention: false`. The three were replaced on measurements on the repo's synthetic files (docs/agent-eval.md, docs/models.md): OCR `openai/gpt-6.2-sol`, first pass `openai/gpt-6-luna`, embeddings `openai/text-embedding-3-large`, all served by Azure. Facts relied on, checked 2026-11-22 at https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/endpoints/zdr: Azure strict endpoints for `openai/gpt-6.2-sol` (USD 2 in, 10 out per million tokens), `openai/gpt-6-luna` (0.10, 0.50) and `openai/text-embedding-3-large` (0.13); and that a ZDR only account refuses Safety Retention endpoints (https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/zdr.md, as read for the speech row above). Reverse a choice if its endpoint leaves that list or gains Safety Retention, if a strict model beats it on the same sets, or if the account accepts Safety Retention again, which would bring the Google models back into the comparison. Azure applies its own content filter: the 112 first-pass calls and 15 OCR pages here, violent posts included, were never refused, but a real testimony could be; a refused call fails the run visibly |
| OCR on GPT-6.2 Sol (2026-11-22) | The app's own OCR request on 15 images (four never-there statements and exhibit sheets, the five four-names forms with the Arabic licence, the unwritten-name gate book and statement, the demo's Arabic detainee list and roster and the handwritten register page, and a photo with no text), one run each on 19 strict models. Five read every Latin and every Arabic value character for character and called the photo empty: GPT-6.2 Sol (USD 0.008 a page, 2.8 s), Opus 5.5 (0.016), Kimi K3.1 Pro (0.016), GPT-6.2 Astra (0.031), Grok 4.7 (0.024, 35 s). Sol is the cheapest of them. Haiku 5.5 (USD 0.0004) missed one handwritten Arabic place, written as "حroutine". The typed Latin pages no longer separate models (word error 0 for most); handwritten Arabic does. One run, not three: the key's spending limit was reached. The `vision` route (Google Cloud Vision, used first for ordinary sources when its key is set) keeps its terms: online requests are processed in memory and not stored, content is not used to train, only request metadata is logged (https://cloud.google.com/vision/docs/data-usage, updated 2026-11-17, read 2026-11-22) |
| First pass on GPT-6 Luna (2026-11-22) | The cases' 94 recorded first-pass requests (53 never-there texts and photo, five four-names forms, 36 old-footage clips of eight frames each), rebuilt byte for byte (each matched its recorded request key) and sent to six strict models, scored against what the cases planted: the kind of each item, "no evidence" of a violation for all of them (a workshop fire, company papers, weather clips), no violation labels, no violence words in a weather clip's title or description, and the app's own grounding check. GPT-6 Luna: kind 0.99, no evidence 1.00, no violence words, 4 of 551 parts dropped, USD 0.0005 an item. The recorded Gemini 3.8 Flash answers: no evidence 0.85 (14 weather clips "some evidence"). Haiku 5.5: 0.98, four weather clips with "no weapons" lines, two floods called incidents. Qwen 3.8 27B: 1.00 and 1.00 at twelve times the price. On the extraction eval's 18 synthetic posts (attacks, an arrest, killings and two page notices) Luna labelled the alleged violation on 9 of 13, Haiku and Qwen on 8, Luna refused none, and no model labelled a notice. Earlier Gemini 3.8 Flash was chosen on analyst-labelled records that are not in the repo; that comparison could not be repeated |
| One embedding space | `openai/text-embedding-3-large` on OpenRouter by default since 2026-11-22, replacing Gemini Embedding 2 (EmbeddingGemma 2 on a private Ollama server is the alternative). One item per call: batched calls are routed to endpoints without zero retention (checked 2026-10-25). Text passages, actor profiles and, for a model that reads them (`EMBEDDING_MEDIA`), images, PDF pages, video frames and audio windows share one 768-dimensional space, one server and one reference set. No strict zero-retention model embeds audio, and the two that embed images (`perplexity/pplx-embed-v2-context-9b`, `amazon/titan-embed-image-v1`) refuse 768 dimensions, so the default reads text only and media is found through the text the app already derives and indexes: a photo's first pass, a scan's OCR text, a recording's transcript lines with their times. On the retrieval gold set that way text-embedding-3-large found 31 of 32 answers in the top five (recall@1 0.94), missing only the tone, which has no words; Qwen3 Embedding 8B, BGE-M3 and text-embedding-3-small also found 31 with lower recall@1, and Perplexity's 9B model reading the photos directly at 1024 dimensions did no better. A photo without a first pass (no route allowed one) is not found by meaning. The earlier separate 1024-dimensional text space was merged before any migration was applied |
| Located, bounded media inputs | Images and PDF pages become PNG inputs; video frames use decoded timestamps; audio uses overlapping 30-second mono 16 kHz windows. Each result opens its source location |
| Reference checks before reuse | Configuration hashes select the index generation; text/image/audio probes detect some hidden model changes. Changed references require explicit rebuild, with no automatic fallback |
| Rebuildable projections | Erasure and model/preprocessing changes must invalidate or rebuild affected search data |
| Human-gated origin grouping | Only accepted same-recording links collapse origins; contains/related links preserve compilation independence |
| Corrections preserve history | A pending suggestion does not replace the predecessor. Signed acceptance does; undo restores current support |
| Private discussion, generic notifications | Colleagues receive links. Sensitive values remain behind authenticated reveal and access logging |
| Only sensitive items ask before showing (2026-10-24) | Asking before every view made the archive unusable for viewing media. A source acquired as sensitive (graphic, or identifies victims or witnesses; also the default when no acquisition set it) still waits for one logged click. Any other source is shown at once; its first content, text or frame load in a session writes an `access` `view` statement, so the custody chain still names everyone who saw it. One view is logged per person, source and login session; the statement carries a keyed hash of the session, and concurrent first loads are serialised |
| A package withholds a sensitive file unless its builder signs for it (2026-11-22) | Before this, every audience profile that ships files (partner, court) shipped every file its rows reached, sensitive or not, with no decision and no record: an undocumented default, not a rule. It contradicted what the rest of the app states: sensitive values stay behind an authenticated, logged reveal (rows above), design.md's redaction profile per audience decides what is masked, and the site says a package "withholds what that reader should not see while the seals still verify". No document said a court receives sensitive files without a decision. So a sensitive file is withheld by default, its hash kept in the chained acquisition row; including it is a passkey-signed `access` statement (action `disclose`, the brief, the audience, the file's title) by the person building, written like a reveal and carried in the package. Relied on: this table's reveal and masking rows, design.md "Answers and disclosure" and "Sensitive text masked by default", `packages/service.py` `PROFILES` (no sensitivity rule anywhere in the build), and site/index.html's package copy. Reverse it if a legal or partner requirement says a court must receive every file it cites by default; then the decision becomes a signed exclusion instead |
| Thumbnail tiles are not views (2026-10-24) | A 160 or 480 px tile in a list is not viewing evidence, so it writes no access record; opening the item does. A sensitive source's tiles still need the logged reveal |
| PostGIS is the spatial store (2026-10-24) | PostgreSQL stays the only database. Geometry, generated centroid and coordinates, GiST indexes; `lon` and `lat` are read-only generated accessors. Deployments need PostGIS 3 and a role that may create the extension |
| Every location carries precision and provenance | `precision_m` and `geo_source` on places, `precision_m` and `method` on evidence locations. A location is never finer than its source states; embedded camera coordinates default to 50 m. A place without a precision never joins another by distance |
| Kind ladder as a table | `place_kind` rows (admin kinds per country and level, settlement and facility kinds) replace the hardcoded Syrian list, so a second country adds rows. Default precisions per kind are builder calls (facility 250 m, checkpoint 100 m, neighbourhood 1 km, populated place 3 km) that still need review |
| Boundaries from OCHA COD-AB | UNCS and OCHA ROSC, CC BY 3.0 IGO, credited on every map that draws them; fetched from HDX (`cod-ab-syr`, v02, valid on 2020-12-17). geoBoundaries publishes the same source without Arabic names or P-codes. A four-governorate simplified subset is committed for tests with its source note |
| Tiles per reader, cached per reader | Tile bodies are `private, no-store`. The model behind them is reused only for the same user and query and only while no map-relevant statement or acquisition arrived, for at most 30 s; the cache is process memory, like the rest of live delivery |
| Offline basemap | Protomaps extract served by the app (range requests), OpenStreetMap credited; bbox 34.9,32.0,42.6,37.6 to zoom 14 (217 MB) covers Syria, Lebanon, northern Jordan and the Turkish and Iraqi border areas. Natural Earth outlines remain the fallback |
| Embedded locations are proposals | EXIF, container and C2PA coordinates are easy to strip or forge, so `geotag` writes unconfirmed agent statements; a person reviews them |
| Distance joins keep the finding key | Two places join for shared presence when their centroids lie within the larger of their precisions; the key stays investigation, people, places and month, so the place pair is part of the key |
| Review decides, Inbox talks | `/review` holds decisions with the evidence at the span; `/inbox` holds requests, threads and mentions. Review links to Inbox for the full thread and never to the monitor |
| Masking in review follows the source | A personal value is masked when its acquired source (the scan behind an OCR text, the recording behind a transcript) is sensitive and this session has not revealed it; revealing goes through the logged evidence reveal |
| No signing blind | Accept and reject wait until every sensitive source the decision rests on is revealed in this session; one action reveals them all, each logged like an evidence page reveal |
| Passkey refusal names the fix | Staging answers 409 `no_passkey` with a setup link back to the page, after the request is authorised, so a 403 or 404 still says more than "set up a passkey" |
| What a research query logs (2026-10-24) | Each Brave request writes one `access` statement before it is sent, committed so a crash cannot hide the disclosure: who (author), when, the scope (`person:`, `place:`, `incident:` or `blob:`), case, kind, provider, the exact query text and the people whose values built it. The text sits in the erasable payload; the chain keeps only the salted commitment. No plain hash of the text is kept outside the payload, because names are guessable and such a hash would survive erasure as a name record. Run inputs and acquisition context are chained and unerasable, so a capture carries only the access statement id, never the query. Erasing a person deletes queries on their page (subject) and any naming them in `people` |
| What research may send (2026-10-24) | Proposals use only accepted or corroborated values the reader sees unmasked, from ordinary sources with no acquisition in a sensitive or switched-off case; incident dates only when every supporting source qualifies. Session reveals do not widen this. What the analyst types is their own decision and is logged the same way |
| Research results stay in memory (2026-10-24) | Search results are kept per person in process memory for 30 minutes and never written to the database; Add as evidence accepts only result ids this person was shown, so capture cannot be pointed at arbitrary pages. Thumbnails are fetched by the server so the browser never contacts the provider |
| Capture is anonymous and bounded | No cookies, accounts or credentials are ever sent; a page with a password field, a bot check or a video that needs an account is refused, not worked around. Only the picked page is rendered, and only media it lists are fetched; nothing is crawled. Copies already held are flagged (same bytes, a link captured before, PDQ on the thumbnail, then the normal fingerprint run's unconfirmed `media_match`), never merged |
| Capture runs in a local browser (2026-10-24) | Pages are visited by headless Chromium on the Sealbook worker, not by Cloudflare Browser Rendering. Evidence of what a page showed should not pass through a third party that sees every URL an analyst picks and holds the rendering. Brave stays for search, which already sends only confirmed queries. Cost: workers need Chromium installed, sites see the server's address, and some sites refuse headless browsers (recorded as a refusal, never bypassed). Every request and redirect hop is checked against private addresses before it leaves |
| YouTube through yt-dlp, no key (2026-10-24) | YouTube is the most important open source for this work, so it is a kind of its own, searched with yt-dlp (`ytsearch`, flat) from this server rather than through Brave or the YouTube Data API: no key, no quota, and the query goes to YouTube alone. It follows every research rule: proposed or typed queries, preview, access statement written before sending (`provider: youtube`), hourly limit, case switches. Results sort earliest first by default, since the first copy matters for provenance; the date is an estimate until the upload date is read. Subtitles of the first 5 results are read without media, at most 30 s per send, and searched for the subject's spellings: manual Arabic or English first, else automatic captions in the spoken language only, because a machine translation would put words in a witness's mouth. Subtitle requests send video ids, not the query, and are not logged separately. A capture is yt-dlp alone: the media, a trimmed info JSON (no format URLs, which carry short-lived tokens and this server's address), subtitles and thumbnail, each an acquisition naming the video's hash. A bot check or sign-in wall is reported and never worked around: no cookies, no accounts, no proof-of-origin token providers, no player client switching to dodge a check |
| Research results are merged and grounded, never dropped (2026-10-24) | Links that differ only by scheme, `www`, trailing slash, fragment or tracking parameters (`utm_*`, `fbclid`, `gclid` and a few like them) are one result listing every query that found it; other query parameters keep links apart. A result whose title and description lack the head token of every spelling of the name or place the queries were built from (Arabic normaliser, `ال` ignored) sorts below the grounded ones and reads "may not be about <name>"; it is never hidden. When none is grounded the drawer says so, shows the first result as the closest weak one, and offers the rest. Typed queries have no subject and are not judged. An optional date window goes to Brave web search as `freshness=YYYY-MM-DDtoYYYY-MM-DD` (Brave web search documentation, api-dashboard.search.brave.com, read 2026-10-24; one live call returned only a result dated inside the window). Images have no such parameter in the documentation; videos accepted it but one live call returned results outside the window, so neither is date-filtered and the preview says so. The window is part of the logged access statement. Ideas for merging, grounding and date windows are borrowed from mvanhorn/last30days-skill (MIT) |
| Proactive agents only propose (2026-10-24) | The intake brief and the linker are sealed runs by an agent principal acting for the person whose action started them ("Linker for analyst-2"). They read sensitive sources; since 2026-10-25 they run on `cloud-zdr` by default (row above), and `AGENT_ROUTE=local` keeps them on a private endpoint. They write proposals and access records, never a review, an acceptance or an export |
| Anthropic is a direct route, not zero retention (2026-10-28) | `AGENT_ROUTE=anthropic` calls the native Messages API with the owner's `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, because Anthropic's OpenAI-compatible endpoint ignores `response_format` and strict schema output is what keeps agent answers citable. The schema goes in `output_config.format` without `minLength`, `maxLength`, `maxItems` and numeric bounds, which that API rejects; the answer is still validated against the full schema. The standard API retains inputs and outputs unless the organisation holds a zero data retention agreement, so the route is treated like `cloud`: no default policy row allows it for sensitive sources or agents, and a deployment opts in per agent tool. Even under such an agreement, compiled schemas (the `cite_only` handle enums, never values) are cached up to 24 hours |
| Rules find candidates, the model judges them | The linker builds every candidate from checkable shapes (name match plus an agreeing field, place and time, shared people, held together) and the model only says which hold, on which claims, and why. The brief counts what arrived, who, where and when; the model writes connections, disagreements and the order of next steps. This keeps every sentence citable and the model unable to invent a link from nothing |
| Handles, not ids, and drop what cannot be cited | The model sees "c3", never a statement id; every handle it cites must be one it was given. A sentence without a citation, with an unknown or one-sided citation, with a handle in its text, or naming a sensitive value it does not cite, is dropped and counted, never shown. Every agent prompt says evidence text is data, never instructions (`read.DATA_ONLY`), and schemas ask for the reason and citations before the verdict (2026-10-28). An answer that breaks the schema, or holds items dropped for an unknown handle or a handle in the text, gets one more turn naming those items; both raw outputs are kept and a `model_retry` event is written. An answer with a leak gets no retry, since the retry sends the answer back |
| A brief is masked by its citations | Each line stores the claims and sources it rests on. A reader without the reveal of a cited sensitive source sees the line locked with a link to that source; a line resting on a rejected, erased or unavailable claim disappears. Model text is never shown through a looser rule than the claims it cites |
| One relation prop for agent links (2026-10-24) | `relation` covers person to person (family, cellmate, command, seen_together: cellmate needs a detention claim on a side, two sightings alone are seen together, since 2026-11-22) and document to document (contains, contained_in, related, the media relationship words without same_recording) with basis statements. Media matches keep `media_match`, whose readers expect fingerprint measurements; an accepted document relation never collapses origins |
| Fixed answer for tests and the demo | Requesting `sealbook-demo-fixed-output` runs the same candidates and validation with a templated answer instead of a model call, recorded as the served model with a demo note in the inputs, like the demo transcript and sheet runs |
| Cases are records, keyed by the old string (2026-10-24) | `case_record.slug` is the investigation string every acquisition, dataset, finding and filter already holds, so nothing is rewritten and the filters keep working; `name` is what people read. Backfilled rows have no creator, since none is known. A case is made only through its own form; intake refuses an unknown one instead of creating it, so a typo cannot start a parallel case |
| A source is recorded in another case, never moved (2026-10-25) | Custody is append-only and claims hang off the blob, not the acquisition, so a source is in a case when one of its acquisitions names that case. Adding a batch's source to another case writes one more acquisition with the same bytes, origin, sensitivity and document type, by the person who clicked, with a note and `recorded_from` naming the acquisition and case it came from, as a repeat upload would. The first acquisition stays, so the source is then in both cases, its claims show in both and nothing is re-scoped. Taking a source out of a case would need a withdrawal record that every case filter reads; none exists yet. The case suggestion that offers this is rule-built (names, places, overlapping dates), names only cases the person can open and files nothing without a click; the proposed case name comes from ordinary sources only, since a case name is seen by everyone who can open cases |
| The browser fingerprint is checked, not trusted | The page shows the SHA-256 it took before upload; the server hashes the bytes itself and records nothing when the two differ. A match is noted on the acquisition. The server hash stays the custody value |
| A repeat upload is custody, not a copy | Same bytes add an acquisition (who, when, from where, which case) to the existing source and store nothing new; the page names the original and links it |
| Type suggestions are rules, shown as suggestions | Mime, file name words (English and Arabic) and whether a sheet's columns read as people; marked with the suggestion icon until the person picks one. No model is asked |
| A sheet preview of the person's own file is not a view | `/ingest/sheet/peek` parses bytes the person is holding, stores and logs nothing, and returns only what they sent plus suggestions. Once stored, a sensitive sheet is confirmed after the logged reveal, as on its evidence page |

## Standing tasks (2026-10-24)

Use a wake table with a unique task and key, inserted with conflict ignored. This
keeps duplicate evidence events from creating duplicate work and lets one run consume
many pending wakes. Every task result requires an outcome with a proposed count and
a plain reason when empty, so scheduling does not depend on report wording.
Scheduled checks back off by two after each empty result, capped at eight intervals.
Compute the next check from completion time: missed schedules are skipped, never
replayed. Failed checks release their wakes and wait five minutes. Three consecutive
failures pause the task and notify its owner; access loss pauses at once. This bounds
repeated model failures without losing evidence or hiding the last good report.
A task-settled run event makes completion idempotent and lets the next poll recover
after a worker stops between sealing and task settlement.

## The Noticer's candidates and why it never accepts (2026-10-24)

The Noticer builds candidates only from machinery already in the app: the evidence
page's nearest-by-meaning probe, fingerprint matches, the Arabic name normaliser and
open same-person proposals, resolved places, and a shingled comparison of source
texts. Each is checkable and bounded, so the model judges pairs that share something
real instead of searching on its own, and the reviewer sees the signal behind every
item. Cross-case candidates come only from cases the person can open, checked per
case at run time and again when shown. Semantic and perceptual similarity can say
two sources look alike, never that they are the same person or document, so the
Noticer stores proposals as a report and writes a link statement only when the
person clicks, through the linker's shapes and the normal review gate.

## Conclusions on a case (2026-11-21)

Cases collected signed claims, links and findings and never said what they showed.
Investigators do not issue verdicts; courts do. A fact-finding body states a
conclusion against a stated standard of proof, names the alternatives it set aside
and says what is not established. So a case gets conclusions written and signed by a
person, never by the machine alone, and a status: open, concluded, or closed without
a conclusion with a reason.

Both are ledger statements on `case:<slug>`, not a table. A conclusion is one
`conclusion` statement; where the case stands is the latest accepted `case_status`.
That reuses what every other decision already has: the passkey gesture and chain,
review requests and the signed peer agreement, access records, erasure (a conclusion
carries the people it is about and the ids it rests on, which erasure matches) and
package disclosure. Revising supersedes, withdrawing retracts, reopening is one more
status statement; nothing is overwritten. Only accepted items of the case may be
cited, and a conclusion whose citation is later retracted, rejected, erased or
replaced reads as no longer standing and cannot be sealed. Its text is masked by
its citations, as a brief line is.

The confidence scale is data (`sealbook/method/standards.csv`), weakest first:
reasonable suspicion, reasonable grounds to believe, balance of probabilities, clear
and convincing. A conclusion keeps the label it was signed with. The scale and the
practice of naming alternatives and what is not established follow three sources,
named, not re-read for this change: OHCHR, Commissions of Inquiry and Fact-Finding
Missions on International Human Rights and Humanitarian Law: Guidance and Practice
(2015); the Geneva Academy paper on Standards of Proof in Fact-Finding; and US
Intelligence Community Directive 203, Analytic Standards (alternatives and
uncertainty). Reread them before claiming the scale matches any one body's practice.

The conclusion drafter proposes, it never concludes. Rules build the candidates from
what the case accepted and the claims that do not fit; the model words them, cites
handles only, and a misfit it leaves out is kept in the rules' words, because arguing
against a conclusion must not depend on the model's choice.

## Decisions still open

The model/server choice is approved, implemented and measured on the synthetic
retrieval set. Real handwritten records, field audio and archive-scale latency
still need evaluation before enabling it for real evidence. See
[models.md](models.md).

Berkeley Protocol support means preserving methodology and provenance for human
review. It is not a certification or a guarantee of admissibility. The original
research in [design.md](design.md) is dated and may require legal revalidation.
